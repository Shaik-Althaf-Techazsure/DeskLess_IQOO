The problem

The report still happens at a desk.

Buildings, campuses, wards, malls and offices are checked on foot. The report is typed up hours later, from memory, by the person who should already be on the next round.

Notes retyped by handPaper notes and memory become typed text hours later.
Photos sorted at nightDozens of pictures get matched to the wrong issue.
No network insideBasements, plant rooms and wards rule out cloud tools.
Reports arrive lateThe people who must fix things wait for the write-up.

How it works

One picture, one sentence. The phone does the rest.

Four steps, all of them on the phone, none of them needing a signal.

  1. 1

    Photograph it, say it

    Point the phone at the problem, take a picture or a short clip, and describe it aloud the way you would to a colleague.

  2. 2

    The phone writes the entry

    Whisper turns the speech into English text. Gemma reads the text and the picture together, fills in what is wrong, where, how severe, who owns it and by when, and says so when the picture does not show what the note says.

  3. 3

    The walk adds up

    Every issue is counted by severity and placed on a map drawn from the phone's own position fixes. No map tiles, no network.

  4. 4

    Leave with the report

    One tap lays out an English PDF with every picture beside its entry, ready before you are back at the gate.

Then it travels, with a network or without

To the laptop

Save for Office Kit puts the PDF and its pack where iQOO Office Kit's file transfer picks them up, and the action items go on the clipboard the phone shares with the laptop.

From phone to phone

With no network at all, the report hops through nearby phones running DeskLess. Each copy is sealed for one phone: the phones in between carry it and cannot read it. A signed receipt comes back.

To the office

When the phone finds a network, the walk and its report go to the company's server by themselves. Issues show on the dashboard as they are raised, before the report is even built.

What comes out

A report somebody can act on.

The pages shown are from a report the app built during testing. Nothing in it was typed at a desk. A report carries:

  • A briefing at the top, written by the model: the state of the place in a sentence and the three issues to do first. Which three is decided by rules, not by the model.
  • A map of the issues, numbered and coloured by severity, with a scale bar and north.
  • Every entry complete: picture, type, severity, location, owner, due date, position, what the phone's sensors read, and the AI's opinion of the picture.
  • Before and after, where a repair was photographed and compared.
  • Signed with a fingerprint. A changed copy no longer matches its signature.
  • A pack beside it: the issues as a spreadsheet, the deadlines as a calendar file, and a task list for each owner.

What is in the app

More than a camera and a form.

AI that stays on the phone

Whisper small and Gemma 4 E2B, both open models, run on the phone's own chip. No cloud call, no cost per report.

Speak the way you speak

Hindi, English or the mix of both that people really use. The report comes out in English.

A second pair of eyes

The model says what it sees in the picture and flags a note that does not fit it. It is an opinion: the inspector can overrule it with one tap.

Film it instead

A clip of up to 20 seconds with the voice beside it. Three stills from it go to the model and one goes in the report.

The phone as an instrument

Level, noise and vibration at a tap; compass, light and steps stamped on every issue; NFC tags that name a place. A guide, not a calibrated meter, and the report says so.

Where, without a map service

Each issue carries the phone's satellite position, and the map is drawn from those alone.

A review before it leaves

Pictures, positions, the order of capture and the sensor readings are checked against the words. The verdict is printed in the report, with each check, and says it is not proof.

Signed by a person

A key kept in the Android Keystore signs the finished PDF, and it works only with a fingerprint.

Ask DeskLess

“What should be fixed first?” Ask about the walk aloud. The answer is worked out on the phone and read back.

A relay with no network

Nearby phones pass a sealed report along until it reaches the one it is for. Scan a QR code once to link two phones.

Was it really fixed?

A second picture after the repair is compared with the first by the model. In the office, the manager sees both before closing the job.

Expiry dates, read

In a medication audit the model reads the date printed on the pack and raises expired stock by itself.

The office

A live console for every role.

Found, given out, repaired, checked, closed. Each person sees their part of that, as it happens, in any browser. The pictures show a sample company.

The whole company on one page: what needs you first, the assistant's ranking, which technician has room, what the numbers say. Tap to see the whole console.

Every company, who is online, sign-ups waiting, and the health of the server. The administrator approves each new company and hands out no work.

Open an issue and the assistant has already ranked it, named who could take it and why, and proposed the next step as one button.

On a phone, in the browser: the technician's jobs, most pressing first. A repair is marked fixed with a photo, and the manager checks it against the first one.

InspectorWalks, reports, and watches what happens to each issue.
TechnicianGets the job with its picture, repairs it, proves it with a photo.
ManagerApproves the team, gives issues out, checks repairs and closes them.
Managing DirectorSees every team side by side and where the risk is.
AdministratorRuns the platform: companies, accounts, the server.

The assistant

It reads every issue so nobody has to read them all.

Each issue gets a rank from 0 to 100, up to three people who could take it with the reasons, and a next step. When something pressing sits untaken, or a job runs past its day, the assistant asks the right person by itself and takes it one level up if nothing moves.

The ranking and the suggestions are fixed rules anyone can read. A language model is used only to word summaries, and it runs on the company's own server.

  • Ranks by severity, deadline, how long it has waited and what the note says
  • Suggests who has room, who knows the place, who is on time
  • Passes on an issue between roles with a note, and keeps the thread
  • Follows up on its own rounds; each company can switch that off

Under the hood

Open models, one Android app, one command.

The app
Native Android: Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, CameraX
Speech to English
Whisper small through sherpa-onnx, with Silero voice detection for long notes
Picture and note to entry
Gemma 4 E2B through LiteRT-LM, on the phone's GPU
Phone to phone
Google Nearby Connections; each report sealed with ECDH P-256 and AES-256-GCM
The report
Android's own PDF writer; ECDSA signature from the Android Keystore
The server
FastAPI and PostgreSQL; dashboards in plain JavaScript, no build step
The assistant
Rules for rank and suggestions; Gemma 3 1B through llama.cpp, optional, for wording
Shipping it
Docker brings up everything with one command; GitHub Actions builds the app on every push
