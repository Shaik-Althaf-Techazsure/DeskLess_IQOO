The problem

Everyone sees it. Nobody reports it.

India runs on shared places: housing societies, colleges, hospitals, offices, markets, factories. What goes wrong in them is seen by many people every day and reported by few, because reporting is slow, has to be typed, wants English, and nobody answers. The people whose job it is to check do the walking by day and the writing by night.

The stuck liftEverybody walks past it. Each assumes someone else has already complained.

How it works

From a sentence to a repair, in four steps.

Whoever notices starts it. Nobody has to type, and nobody has to chase.

  1. 1

    Someone says it

    A resident, a student, a nurse, a guard: they tap the microphone and say what is wrong in their own words. An inspector on a round adds a picture or a short clip. The phone reads the entry back and waits for “send it”.

  2. 2

    The phone writes it

    Whisper turns the speech into English text. Gemma reads it with the picture and fills in what, where, how severe, who and by when. If something is missing it asks one question aloud. What nobody said is left out.

  3. 3

    It lands on the right desk

    The office's assistant ranks every issue and names who could take it. On My desk the phone reads the work aloud and the manager answers by voice: “Give the first one to Arjun by Monday.” Nothing is done until it has been shown and confirmed.

  4. 4

    Fixed, with proof

    The technician photographs the repair. The phone compares it with the first picture, the manager closes the job, and the phone of the person who reported it says so.

And when there is no network at all

Phone to phone, sealed

A report hops through nearby phones running DeskLess until it reaches the one it is for. The phones in between carry it and cannot read it, and a signed receipt comes back. What a user tells with no network is handed to a nearby phone of the place, which sends it on.

Walkie-talkie

Hold, speak, let go. The voice travels over the same sealed link to every linked phone within Bluetooth and Wi-Fi reach, or onward through a chain of DeskLess phones.

To the laptop, then the office

Save for Office Kit puts the report where iQOO Office Kit's file transfer picks it up. When the phone finds a network, everything goes to the company's server by itself.

The product

See it working.

These are the app's own screens: some from the iQOO 15, the rest from the Android emulator during the build, test pictures and all. The consoles show a sample company. Tap one to see it whole.

DeskLess User: say it, and you are done.

For residents, students, staff and visitors. A name, an email address and a code are all it takes to join: no forms, no approval. Pick the kind of place and the place, then talk.

  • Read back before it goes. “Make it high”, “it is block C, not block B”, “send it”, “forget it”.
  • A question is a question. “Is the cafeteria open on Sunday?” goes to be answered, not repaired.
  • You hear back. Who has it, what they answered, and when it was repaired, checked and closed.

My desk: the office speaks, and is answered by voice.

Whoever the work now waits on opens My desk and is told, aloud, what waits for them. They answer the same way, and the phone shows what it understood before anything happens.

  • “Give the first one to Arjun by Monday.” It goes to that technician, with that day.
  • “I fixed the first one.” The camera opens, and the repair is compared with the picture it was reported with.
  • “Close job 5.” Or send it back, with what is still wrong.
  • One phone, several people. A shared phone becomes yours with a tap on your name.

The phones talk to each other.

Scan a QR code once and two phones are linked. From then on reports, voice and a user's entries cross between them over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi, with no internet and no mobile signal.

  • Sealed for one phone. A phone that carries a report for another cannot open it.
  • Delivered and opened. The sender watches each copy go from waiting to handed over.
  • Honest about reach. It is a walkie-talkie in how it is used, not a radio set: some tens of metres, or further through phones in between.

For the inspection round

A report somebody can act on.

The pages shown are from a report the app built during testing. Nothing in it was typed at a desk. A report carries:

  • A briefing at the top, written by the model: the state of the place in a sentence and the three issues to do first. Which three is decided by rules, not by the model.
  • A map of the issues, numbered and coloured by severity, drawn from the phone's own position fixes.
  • Every entry complete: picture, type, severity, location, owner, due date, what the phone's sensors read, and the AI's opinion of the picture.
  • Before and after, where a repair was photographed and compared.
  • Signed with a fingerprint. A changed copy no longer matches its signature.
  • A pack beside it: the issues as a spreadsheet, the deadlines as a calendar file, a task list for each owner.

The office

Nothing waits for someone to remember it.

Found, given out, repaired, checked, closed. Each person sees their part of that as it happens, on their phone and in any browser.

The assistant

It reads every issue so nobody has to read them all.

Each issue gets a rank from 0 to 100, up to three people who could take it with the reasons, and a next step. When something pressing sits untaken, or a job runs past its day, the assistant asks the right person by itself and takes it one level up if nothing moves.

The ranking and the suggestions are fixed rules anyone can read. A language model is used only to word summaries, and it runs on the company's own server.

  • Ranks by severity, deadline, how long it has waited and what the note says
  • Suggests who has room, who knows the place, who is on time
  • Passes on an issue between roles with a note, and keeps the thread
  • Follows up on its own rounds; each company can switch that off

AI you can check

The model words it. Plain code decides.

A small model on a phone will sometimes be wrong. DeskLess is built so that being wrong cannot do damage.

Nothing made upA name, a place or a date that nobody said is kept out of the report, and shown to you as kept out. Every field says where it came from.
Shown before it is doneA spoken instruction becomes a list of changes on the screen. Nothing changes until you tap Do it, and you can put it back.
An opinion, never a verdictThe picture check, the repair check and the review can each be overruled by the person on the spot, and the report says so.
Measured every timeTwenty fixed notes run through the same models on the phone after every change: fields right, anything made up, seconds, battery.

Speech is turned into words on the phone, and the recording stays there. What is sent is the written entry with its pictures, and only to your organisation's own server.

What is in the app

More than a microphone and a form.

AI that stays on the phone

Whisper small and Gemma 4 E2B, both open models, run on the phone's own chip. No cloud call, no cost per report.

Speak the way you speak

Hindi, English or the mix people really use, written down in English. Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi and Bengali can be chosen too and are still being tested.

The phone asks back

When a note leaves out where, who or by when, the phone asks one question aloud, listens, and fills only those fields.

Ask it, or tell it

“What should be fixed first?” is answered from the walk. “Mark issue 3 high, give it to Suresh” is shown as changes, then done.

A second pair of eyes

The model says what it sees in the picture and flags a note that does not fit it. A clip of up to 20 seconds works too.

The phone as an instrument

Level, noise and vibration at a tap; compass, light and steps stamped on every issue; NFC tags that name a place. A guide, not a calibrated meter.

Where, without a map service

Each issue carries the phone's satellite position, and the map of the walk is drawn from those alone.

A review before it leaves

Pictures, positions, the order of capture and the readings are checked against the words. The verdict is printed, and says it is not proof.

Signed by a person

A key kept in the Android Keystore signs the finished PDF, and it works only with a fingerprint.

It learns your people and places

A name heard a little wrong is spelt the way it is known, and known places are one tap away. No model involved: one small file on the phone.

Two apps, one system

DeskLess for the team that looks after a place, DeskLess User for the people who use it. Built from one code base.

Expiry dates, read

In a medication audit the model reads the date printed on the pack and raises expired stock by itself.

Under the hood

Open models, one Android app, one command.

The app
Native Android: Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, CameraX. Two editions from one code base.
Speech to English
Whisper small through sherpa-onnx, with Silero voice detection for long notes
Words and picture to entry
Gemma 4 E2B through LiteRT-LM, on the phone's GPU, with plain code checking what it returns
Phone to phone
Google Nearby Connections; every report and voice message sealed with ECDH P-256 and AES-256-GCM
The report
Android's own PDF writer; ECDSA signature from the Android Keystore
The server
FastAPI and PostgreSQL; consoles and the live linkage in plain JavaScript, no build step
The assistant
Rules for rank, routing and follow-up; Gemma 3 1B through llama.cpp, optional, for wording
Shipping it
Docker brings up everything with one command; GitHub Actions tests the server and builds both apps on every push
